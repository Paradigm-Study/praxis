# 05 — Brand Principles (The Eight Non-Negotiables)

These define Praxis in explicit opposition to Meta MCI / Colleague Skill / Surge. **Publish them on day one** — they do the marketing.

1. **Experts always own their data.** Export keys, deletion rights, profile portability — even from already-trained adapters where technically possible.

2. **Royalties always flow.** Every commercial use generates attribution and revenue share back to the contributing expert(s). 60–70% to the expert. No exceptions.

3. **No employer-deployed capture without expert consent.** We refuse enterprise contracts that don't include per-expert opt-in and individual royalty rights — even at Fortune 500 prices. This policy is public.

4. **AI-lab licensing is a separate opt-in.** Opting in to mentorship does not opt an expert into foundation-lab data sales. Distinct consent, distinct contract.

5. **Transparent dataset provenance.** Every dataset sold lists contributing experts (or aggregates with cardinality), episode counts, time range, and redaction methodology. Audit-ready.

6. **EU AI Act compliance globally.** Even US-only customers get the EU standard. The compliance overhead is a moat, not a burden. No emotion recognition, no productivity scoring, no anything classifiable as workplace surveillance.

7. **Adversarial robustness as ethics.** We detect anti-distill sabotage — not to punish workers, but to keep datasets honest. Sabotage signals also surface coerced-capture customers, whom we then drop.

8. **"Forget" is real.** One-click panic button erases the last N minutes. Quarterly export includes every captured byte. Revocation works retroactively wherever technically feasible.

## The decision test (carry through everything)

> **"Would an expert proud of their craft hear how we're doing this and choose to participate?"**

Yes → Praxis. "Only if I had to" → Meta MCI with better fonts.

## The press answer

When a journalist asks: *"Aren't you just doing what Meta is doing, with better PR?"*

> *"We capture similar data. Where we differ is in three ways that matter: every expert chose to be here and signed a paper saying so; every dollar of revenue flows back to them in proportion to their contribution; and they can pull their data out tomorrow. Meta employees can't say yes or no, can't earn from their work, and can't leave with what they made. Same data flow, completely different transaction. We think this version produces better data anyway, because nobody sabotages something they own."*

It survives press scrutiny, regulator scrutiny, and — most importantly — the expert's own scrutiny at install time. It's defensible because it's true.
