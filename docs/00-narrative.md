# 00 — Narrative & Positioning

## The narrative

> **Every expert carries decades of judgment that dies when they retire — or gets extracted by their employer right before they're laid off. Praxis is the opposite. You capture your craft on your own terms, while you work. You publish what you choose. AI mentors trained on your taste teach the next generation, in their workflow, asynchronously. Every time someone learns from you, you get paid. We are the publishing platform for tacit knowledge — Substack for craft, not Surge for labels.**

Three things this narrative does that nothing else does:

- It puts the **expert as author**, not as subject or product.
- It frames the primary buyer as **the learner**, not the lab — even though labs are a real secondary buyer.
- It explicitly opposes the Meta MCI / Colleague Skill / Surge models, all of which are public villains in the 2026 labor-rights story.

This is a creator-economy story, not an extraction-industry story. Same data, opposite brand.

## What Praxis is NOT

| Anti-position | Why we reject it |
|---|---|
| **Meta MCI** (employer forces capture, then fires) | The dystopia. Worker resistance, EU illegality, brand poison. |
| **Colleague Skill** (distill the departing coworker to replace them) | Same problem, different geography. Worker is subject, not author. |
| **Surge / Scale** (pay piecework for labels) | Top experts won't show up. Hawthorne effect contaminates the signal. |
| **Paperboy / AirJelly** (capture for the expert's own AI assistant) | Single-player. No publishing layer, no royalty, no compounding flywheel. |
| **Maven / Reforge / Coursera** (explicit course content) | Captures what experts can *articulate* — exactly what they're worst at. Misses the tacit. |

We are the only product where the expert is the **publisher**, the learner is the **customer**, and the lab is the **secondary monetization** — all on consented data.

## The unifying vision

Praxis is not selling to AI labs OR to corporate L&D OR to learners. It is the **rights-cleared tacit-knowledge layer that every AI tutor, training platform, and edtech tool plugs into.**

Analogy stack, in increasing fidelity:
- **Getty Images for expert taste** — we don't shoot the photos (build the tutors); we license the content (taste profiles) they need.
- **Spotify for tacit knowledge** — experts (artists) publish profiles (albums); agents/learners/companies stream them; royalties flow to creators on usage.
- **OpenAI for taste** — a model layer any AI application can call: `praxis.complete(profile="senior_SRE", context=...)`.

The market this addresses is the ~$1.5T of "learning" spend (corporate L&D ~$360B, higher ed ~$700B, bootcamps/skills ~$80B, coaching ~$25B, plus the emerging AI-tutor category) that is all shifting from videos/docs/courses toward AI-driven delivery — and all of it needs tacit content that doesn't exist yet.

## The dual-sided data flywheel

Unlike pure expert-capture, the teaching frame accumulates data from **both** sides:

```
EXPERTS (supply)                 LEARNERS (demand)
────────────────                 ─────────────────
Tacit work patterns        →     Common misconceptions
Edit-in-place diffs        →     Where they get stuck
Reasoning traces           →     Questions that surface insight
"Ugh no" reflexes          →     Pedagogical gradients
Negative-space judgments   →     What examples actually land
       ↓                                ↓
       └──────────────┬───────────────┘
                      ▼
           PRAXIS KNOWLEDGE GRAPH
                      │
       ┌──────────────┼───────────────┐
       ▼              ▼                ▼
  Better expert   Better tutor    Better licensing
    profiles       prompts          datasets
```

Either dataset alone is valuable. Together they are a complete training corpus for the next generation of AI tutors. Khan/Coursera have the demand side; nobody has the supply side; only Praxis has both.

## The decision test

> **"Would an expert proud of their craft hear how we're doing this and choose to participate?"**

This single question adjudicates product, GTM, contract, and capture-design decisions. If the answer is "yes," it's Praxis. If "only if I had to," it's Meta MCI with better fonts.
